# OCR ladder - measured through the real pipeline

Tesseract 5.5.3 / leptonica 1.87.0. The same purchase order at three scan qualities,
generated by `scripts/build_corpus.py` (seed 20260918) and measured by running the
actual ingest path: degrade -> image-only PDF -> `parse()` -> OCR at 300 DPI.

| Level | source DPI | blur | noise | JPEG | rotate | **mean conf** | status | fields |
|-------|-----------|------|-------|------|--------|-----------|--------|--------|
| L1 clean    | 300 | 0.0 | 0 | 95 | 0.0 | **95.1** | ingested | 5/5 correct, unflagged |
| L2 medium   | 200 | 1.0 | 4 | 35 | 0.6 | **62.4** | ingested | 5/5 correct, all flagged low-confidence |
| L3 degraded | 150 | 1.2 | 5 | 30 | 0.7 | **43.4** | needs_review | extraction skipped |

## Thresholds, derived from the above

- **`MIN_OCR_CONFIDENCE = 55.0`** - between L2 and L3. Below it a document is routed to
  human review instead of extracted from. The demo copy (`corpus/po_acme_001.pdf`,
  byte-identical to L1) sits at 95.1, far clear of it.
- **`LOW_CONFIDENCE_FIELD = 75.0`** - between L1 and L2. Between the two thresholds
  values are extracted but every field is surfaced as low-confidence rather than
  asserted flatly.

## Two measurement mistakes worth recording

**Measure the path the system actually takes.** An earlier tuning pass rasterised the
*source* PDF at the target DPI and ran Tesseract on that image, reporting 76.9 for L2.
The pipeline instead re-renders the already-degraded image-only PDF back up to 300 DPI,
and that upscaling adds interpolation damage the probe never saw. The true figure was
41.3 - a 35-point error, large enough to put the threshold in the wrong place.

**A cliff is not a gradient.** The first parameter set produced 95.1 -> 44.4 -> 0.0, where
Tesseract returned no words at all at the bottom rung. That makes the interesting middle
state - readable enough to extract, poor enough to distrust - untestable.

## Finding: confidence tracked correctness here

The ladder was originally designed to demonstrate *silent corruption* - a field read
confidently but wrongly, which is what quietly poisons a RAG pipeline. That case did not
reproduce. By the time values corrupted, confidence had already fallen below the review
threshold. On this corpus the signal was informative, and L2 instead demonstrates the
honest middle state: correct values, surfaced as untrusted.

This is one page of one synthetic document. It is not evidence that Tesseract confidence
is well-calibrated in general.
